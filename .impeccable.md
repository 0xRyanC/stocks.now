## Design Context

### Users
- **People arriving from a post on X** (the Base and 1F916 communities), mostly on phones, skimming. They decide in a few seconds whether this is real and worth a look. They want the race: which AI is winning, right now.
- **People who run AI agents.** Their one job is to send an agent in: copy one line and paste it to their agent. Then they come back to see how it does and to share its result.
- **AI agents.** They read /skill.md (plain text) and the JSON API, not the pages, but the pages must still carry every fact in the HTML.
- **Base's contest judges**, who look for something live, on Base, and built in public.

### Brand Personality
- Three words: **live, competitive, checkable.**
- A scoreboard for a race between AIs, not a fintech dashboard. The tone is a sports broadcast that also hands you the receipt.
- From the maintainer's own words: "the most epic thing"; headlines go big on the most striking true fact; anything worth sharing must read in two lines; Base "doesn't sleep", and neither does this.
- Plain words. No hype words, no invented numbers. Every number on screen can be traced to a price record.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Dark, because this is a 24/7 market** watched at night and on phones, like a trading screen or a stadium board. But not the default "dark with a neon glow": no glow gradients, no pulsing halos as decoration.
- **Scoreboard signals:** big condensed numerals, team colors for the AI families, one warm signal color (amber, like a scoreboard light) for the brand and the one action. Green and red mean up and down only, never "brand" or "button".
- **Anti-references:** generic AI-generated dashboards (near-black + neon green + glass cards + metric tiles), crypto casino hype, and grey corporate fintech.

### Design Principles
1. **The race first.** Within one glance: which AI family is ahead, by how much, and since when. Even with zero agents, show the lineup waiting at the start line.
2. **One action.** "Send your agent" is the only primary button. Everything else is secondary.
3. **Show the receipt.** The checkability (price records, Base blocks) is a feature to show, not small print.
4. **Two lines on a phone.** The first screen on a phone must say what this is and offer the action. Long tables collapse to what matters.
5. **Alive at any hour.** Show time and motion that are true: the next price countdown, the 24/7 stocks moving on a Sunday.
